# Seven slide deck

Official alternative to the video: at most 7 slides. Each slide below gives the headline, the body, and the visual. Export to PDF at 16:9 from the deck source.

## Slide 1: The problem in one picture

Headline: Two numbers decide a south pole landing.

Body: At the pole the Sun never rises more than about 1.5 degrees. Earth stays within about 8 degrees of the horizon. A ridge a few hundred metres high therefore decides whether a site has power and whether it has a radio link.

Visual: the horizon panorama with the Sun just above the silhouette and Earth just below it, no labels except `Sun` and `Earth`.

## Slide 2: The question a planner actually asks

Headline: Which region, on which date, with which worst gap.

Body: The work is a comparison, and today it takes three tools and an afternoon. The challenge asks for that comparison to be fast.

Visual: one line of type, large, on the paper background. No icons.

## Slide 3: What Selenorizon does

Headline: One screen per decision.

Body: Horizon view for one site and instant. Comparison across eight published regions. Date scan for one site. Terrain horizon as a visible input. Everything in the URL.

Visual: four screen thumbnails in a row, each with its route name under it.

## Slide 4: Terrain is an input, not an assumption

Headline: You can see the skyline that produced the number.

Body: The horizon is sixteen sector values, editable by dragging, importable from a CSV profile, and printed in the URL. Three modes are labelled on screen: flat reference, edited, imported. The flat mode is the default so no reading is silently terrain-corrected.

Visual: the sector editor with a dragged handle and the `occluded` label appearing on the Sun marker.

## Slide 5: It is checked against NASA ephemerides

Headline: Residuals, not adjectives.

Body: Sub-solar and sub-Earth selenographic coordinates at 13 epochs from 2026 to 2028, and topocentric azimuth and elevation at two published sites, against JPL Horizons DE441. The table on the Model screen is generated by the validator.

Visual: the accuracy table, largest three rows highlighted.

## Slide 6: What it changes

Headline: From an afternoon to thirty seconds.

Body: A trade study that needed an ephemeris tool, a DEM viewer, and a spreadsheet now happens in one screen and leaves with a CSV. An educator can show why the pole is hard in ten seconds.

Visual: before and after as two timelines, no decorative icons.

## Slide 7: Limits, stated

Headline: What this does not do.

Body: No hazard detection, no link budget, no thermal model, no in-browser DEM ray-march. The horizon profile is the hand-off point from a DEM. Data sources and the model specification are in the repository.

Visual: a short list, then one line with the repository address and the live link.

## Presentation notes

- Never read the slides. Each slide has one number or one image, and the talk carries the rest.
- Open with the panorama, not with the product name.
- If asked what is novel: the terrain array with provenance on every reading, plus published residuals from a client-side engine.
- If asked about the DEM gap: state plainly that the build environment could not reach a DEM, so the hand-off is an importable profile, and the derivation command is documented.
